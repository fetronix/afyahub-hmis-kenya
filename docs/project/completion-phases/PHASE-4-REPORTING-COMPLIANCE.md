## Implemented operational slice

### Facility outpatient encounter / diagnosis register — Partial

The **DEPARTMENTAL / OPD / Diagnosis reports** and **RECORDS / OPD diagnosis reports** catalogue entries now both link to `outpatient-diagnoses`. They deliberately share a limited, transparent register rather than pretending to implement two different statutory aggregations. The other 142 entries have not been converted into completed reports. The current catalogue contains **144 entries: 23 linked to 19 limited engines, one invoice workflow, and 120 unavailable**. See the inventory for every entry's source, delivery, permissions, filters, exports, tests and acceptance dependency.

- **Persisted source and grain.** A tenant/facility-owned `OUTPATIENT` encounter anchors scope. A tenant-matched patient and facility-matched department are joined; diagnoses are left-joined by encounter. Each recorded diagnosis is a separate row; duplicates are preserved. An encounter without a diagnosis is one explicit no-diagnosis row. `total` counts filtered rows, not unique patients, diagnoses, completed visits, morbidity cases or denominators. No deduplication, diagnostic interpretation, recoding or aggregation into official indicators is performed.
- **Privacy/integrity.** For a diagnosis whose patient ID does not match the encounter patient, the new report exposes only an inconsistent-link warning: its diagnosis ID, codes, display, release and other clinical detail are withheld, and the inconsistent diagnosis fields cannot be searched. Missing/out-of-scope patient and department details contribute to a review note. Inconsistent diagnosis links require authorized source review, not automatic repair. The selector includes patients with a recorded same-tenant outpatient encounter at the facility even when registered at a different facility; registration alone is not a qualifying encounter.
- **Dates and lifecycle.** Both encounter-start dates are required; valid calendar dates form an inclusive range of at most **366 days**, implemented as `start >= from` and `start < to + 1 day`. Source `started_at` is a timestamp **without time zone**: no local-zone or UTC origin is inferred. Boundary meaning depends on how the source was recorded and needs facility review. Exact source states appear on rows. The progress filter groups `PLANNED`, `ACTIVE` and `ON_HOLD` as pending, `COMPLETED` as completed, `CANCELLED` as cancelled, and any other/null state as unknown. A recorded state is not independently verified evidence of completed care.
- **Coding.** Recorded generic, ICD-10 and ICD-11 codes/titles and the recorded ICD-11 release remain distinct; missing cells display “Not recorded”. The quality check flags unsupported/missing systems or diagnosis status, absent ICD-10/generic code, absent ICD-11 code/release, and conflicting non-empty generic versus system-specific codes. It does not establish title completeness or validity. Presence/shape or a recorded release is not WHO validation. The report neither backfills a missing release nor converts an old code. Age, sex, programme, standardized case and required national denominators are not fabricated.
- **Access and output.** Server authorization requires `reports.read`, `clinical.read`, `patients.read`, a scoped facility account and enabled analytics/OPD modules; export/print also require `reports.export`. The UI uses real, scoped, searchable paginated patient and department selectors; selected IDs and literal search are revalidated on the server. Source ownership, paging (≤100), offset, allowed status and required date bounds are enforced independently of the browser. An authenticated view/print/export is audited transactionally, with privacy access logs for returned patients; audit failure denies the report/selector data. CSV neutralizes spreadsheet formula leaders, XLSX cells are strings, and PDF fails rather than silently substituting unsupported characters. Print covers the current page; full PDF/XLSX/CSV is refused above 1,000 filtered rows rather than silently truncated. Filters and limitations accompany output. An audit records a request, not physical delivery.
- **Boundaries.** This is a live, facility-local source register, not a national form, case definition, confirmed ICD catalogue, individual clinician attestation, registry submission or payment/claim evidence. Source timestamp provenance, high-volume query performance, source integrity/remediation workflow, privacy review and facility UAT remain open. Patient-selector eligibility establishes a matching encounter, not patient consent or national identity validation.

### Separate clinical diagnosis security concern — unresolved

Inspection of `src/db/queries/clinical.ts:getDiagnosesScoped` shows that the existing encounter-ID read first scopes the encounter but then selects diagnoses by `encounter_id` without an independent `diagnoses.patient_id = encounter.patient_id` condition. If a historical or otherwise inconsistent row points at the scoped encounter while naming another patient, that separate clinical API may return its details. The new report's withholding and search guards do **not** fix or prove safety of that clinical path. `recordDiagnosisScoped` already checks patient/encounter consistency on its write path, but does not establish that all historical rows are consistent. This is a code-inspection finding, not a claim that such records exist; no real patient data was examined. Treat it as a Phase 1/2 security blocker for authorized investigation, scoped negative tests and safe remediation, without rewriting historical evidence.

## WHO ICD-11 and cross-module audit

- WHO's supported-classification page lists English ICD-11 MMS **2026-01** [2](https://icd.who.int/docs/icd-api/SupportedClassifications/); the application default is **2025-01** (`who-icd11-reference.ts`), overridable with a format-checked release. Do not silently upgrade a configured release or rewrite old diagnoses. A release string's format is not evidence that WHO supports it or that the facility/payer has approved it.
- `icd11.service.ts` requests WHO API v2 English MMS search when configured, with bounded search/timeout, and caches returned code/title/release, source and fetch metadata. If unavailable it explicitly returns only previous WHO-provenanced cache results and a warning; this partial cache is not a full release distribution. New ICD-11 diagnoses require a matching active cached WHO result for the specified release and preserve the WHO metadata. New ICD-10 inputs require a code and description but do not thereby prove WHO validation. No WHO credentials, live provider search or real-world terminology acceptance was tested in this phase; tests exercise local/mocked and synthetic paths.
- Historical recorded fields remain distinct in the new report. However `presentPersistedDiagnosis` in the clinical service and the private-claim snapshot in `src/insurance/claims/private-claims.service.ts` classify **every non-ICD-11 value** as ICD-10, including unexpected or missing coding systems; private-claim snapshots do not retain the ICD-11 release. These are unresolved cross-module coding/provenance gaps. The claim path does independently match diagnosis patient to invoice patient, but its ICD fallback and absent release are not repaired here. Audit other FHIR, claim and clinical consumers before relying on aggregate code counts or external exchange.
- The WHO **2025-01** reference guide discusses the classification and mapping arrangements [1](https://icdcdn.who.int/icd11referenceguide/en-2025-01/refguide.pdf), while the current WHO-FIC interoperability hub describes mapping under WHO licensing [1](https://www.who.int/standards/classifications/who-fic-interoperability-hub). Review the current [WHO ICD-11 license](https://icd.who.int/en/docs/ICD11-license.pdf), authoritative release-specific terms and intended use before constructing or distributing a crosswalk. No validated ICD-10→11 mapping, auto-recode or payer acceptance is claimed.
