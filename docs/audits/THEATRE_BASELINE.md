## Integration trace

- **Patients and OPD:** Existing patient lookup/registration and clinical records are in `src/db/queries/patients.ts`, `src/routes/domains/patients.routes.ts`, and `src/clinical/clinical-records.routes.ts`; `theatre_cases.patient_id` references the existing patient. A same-facility patient association must be checked, not inferred from an ID.
- **Inpatient and nursing:** Admissions, wards, transfers and nursing observations already exist in `src/routes/inpatient.routes.ts`, `src/inpatient/`, `src/nursing/nursing.service.ts`, and `src/db/schema/inpatient.ts`. Theatre does not currently establish or perform a ward transfer; a completed case must not discharge an admission.
- **Clinical staff:** Staff/users and role permissions exist in `src/auth/staff-roles/` and the auth schema. The baseline Theatre case records names as free text, not validated staff assignments.
- **Laboratory and radiology:** Existing orders/results are in `src/routes/laboratory.routes.ts`, `src/routes/radiology.routes.ts`, and `src/db/queries/laboratory.ts`. No Theatre-specific association or automatic interpretation was established.
- **Pharmacy, medication and stock:** `src/routes/medications.routes.ts`, `src/routes/pharmacy.routes.ts`, and `src/inventory/inventory.service.ts` provide existing medication and FEFO dispense workflows. No Theatre issue/return/waste integration or theatre-linked medication administration record was traced; prescription is not administration.
- **Billing:** `src/billing/billing.service.ts` uses facility tariffs, explicit billing keys, and idempotent billing items. `src/billing/tariff-codes.ts` has a `PROCEDURE` family but no dedicated Theatre automatic events. Existing Theatre writes do not invoke the billing engine. Prices, surgeon fees and mapping must not be invented.
- **Audit:** `audit_logs` and its existing transaction patterns are available, but baseline Theatre case writes do not add a Theatre-specific clinical or scheduling audit trail.

## Clinical and operational gaps

Preoperative assessment, authorized readiness decisions, surgery-specific consent history, three auditable checklist stages, operation and anaesthesia documentation, controlled amendments, recovery handover, room management, schedule conflict protection, stock issue/return, billing/reversal, and Theatre reports have not been demonstrated by the baseline implementation. A status dropdown or a single JSON checklist is not evidence of a completed procedure or clinical readiness.

The WHO's [Surgical Safety Checklist resources](https://www.who.int/teams/integrated-health-services/patient-safety/research/safe-surgery/tool-and-resources) provide the 2009 checklist and implementation manual and encourage facility consultation for adaptations. Any adapted checklist requires local clinical review; software tests do not establish clinical approval, regulatory compliance or actual team performance.

## Safety boundaries for implementation

1. Reuse `theatre_cases`, the patient/staff records, existing facility authorization and audit infrastructure. Do not backfill invented clinical history or automatically certify legacy checklist values.
2. Never create fictional clinicians, consent, room availability, completed procedures, medication administration, stock movements, prices, invoices or outcomes.
3. Persist auditable changes, block cross-facility access, and use transaction-safe scheduling and idempotency where implemented. Do not silently mutate signed clinical records or posted finance records.
4. Keep unimplemented clinical, stock, billing and transfer actions clearly unavailable rather than presenting placeholders as successful operations.
5. Test with synthetic data in newly created isolated PostgreSQL databases only. The historical chain contains a destructive operation and must never be replayed against existing or production data. Qualified facility personnel must review and approve clinical workflow and checklist adaptation separately.
